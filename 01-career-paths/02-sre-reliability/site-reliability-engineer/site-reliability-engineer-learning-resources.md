# Site Reliability Engineer: Learning Resources

Start with the recurring operating problem, not the automation tool. Connect the service objective and response to an engineering improvement whose effect can be checked.

## Identify recurring operational work

Read [Google SRE: Eliminating Toil](https://sre.google/sre-book/eliminating-toil/), beginning with characteristics of toil and how to identify repeated operational effort.

Record frequency, user impact, required judgment, and the maintenance implications of a possible automation.

## Define service objectives

Read [Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/), beginning with selecting indicators, targets, and the improvement process.

Connect useful service behavior to a target, window, and agreed decision process.

## Make alerts actionable

Read [Google SRE Workbook: Alerting on SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/), beginning with error-budget consumption and alerting trade-offs.

Explain the failure represented, urgency, operator response, and expected evidence.

## Close an incident improvement

Read [Google SRE Workbook: Postmortem Culture](https://sre.google/workbook/postmortem-culture/), beginning with incident analysis, action items, and organizational learning.

Produce a supported timeline and an owned engineering action with a validation condition.

Choose a [project](site-reliability-engineer-labs-and-portfolio-projects.md) when you need execution evidence, or work through the [assessment scenarios](site-reliability-engineer-interview-and-assessment-resources.md) to apply the idea under changed conditions.
