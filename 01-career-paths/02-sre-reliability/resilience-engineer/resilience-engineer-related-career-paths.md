# Resilience Engineer: Related Career Paths

What useful service remains during disruption, and can the system recover without unacceptable data loss? When the next observation points to a different responsibility, use the routes below.

## [Chaos Engineer](../chaos-engineer/README.md)

This route focuses on testing explicit resilience hypotheses through bounded, observable fault experiments. Ask: **Which assumption will the experiment test, what will stop it, and how will you verify recovery?**

Open [architecture resources](../chaos-engineer/chaos-engineer-architecture-resources.md) for the design boundary, or [production practices](../chaos-engineer/chaos-engineer-production-practices.md) when you have an operating observation to investigate.

## [Database Reliability Engineer](../database-reliability-engineer/README.md)

This route focuses on protecting database correctness, availability, performance, and recoverability as connected responsibilities. Ask: **Can you preserve correct data and useful service behavior through overload, replication failure, and restoration?**

Open [architecture resources](../database-reliability-engineer/database-reliability-engineer-architecture-resources.md) for the design boundary, or [production practices](../database-reliability-engineer/database-reliability-engineer-production-practices.md) when you have an operating observation to investigate.

## [Capacity Engineer](../capacity-engineer/README.md)

A recovery can fail because the surviving system cannot absorb returning traffic or queued work. Use capacity resources to assess useful degraded-state capacity, acquisition delay, and demand-management options.

Open [architecture resources](../capacity-engineer/capacity-engineer-architecture-resources.md) for the design boundary, or [production practices](../capacity-engineer/capacity-engineer-production-practices.md) when you have an operating observation to investigate.
