# Service Reliability Engineer: Learning Resources

Define the journey and eligible events before choosing an alert. Study signals and release evidence afterward so the measurements remain tied to the changed user path.

## Define a user-centered indicator

Read [Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/), beginning with selecting indicators, targets, and the improvement process.

Write successful and eligible events for one journey and identify important subpopulations.

## Translate objectives into response

Read [Google SRE Workbook: Alerting on SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/), beginning with error-budget consumption and alerting trade-offs.

Compare urgency, windows, sparse traffic, and actionability for an alert.

## Understand signal roles

Read [OpenTelemetry signals](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/concepts/signals/), beginning with signals relevant to your diagnostic question.

Choose metrics, traces, and logs according to their contribution to measurement or diagnosis.

## Design release acceptance

Read [Canarying Releases](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/), beginning with canary design, comparison signals, and decision criteria.

Define relevant exposure, comparison, stop conditions, and recovery evidence for a changed request path.

Choose a [project](service-reliability-engineer-labs-and-portfolio-projects.md) when you need execution evidence, or work through the [assessment scenarios](service-reliability-engineer-interview-and-assessment-resources.md) to apply the idea under changed conditions.
