# Related careers

DevOps architecture overlaps with several engineering roles because software delivery crosses application, infrastructure, platform, security, and operational boundaries. Use this guide to understand collaboration and transferable responsibilities.

Role titles and authority vary by organization. The comparisons below describe common responsibility patterns, not fixed job definitions or a formal promotion ladder. Linked career folders identify repository locations and may still contain placeholders.

## Collaboration is part of the architecture

An architect should work with application teams on deployability and compatibility, testing specialists on useful evidence, security teams on trust and threat assessment, platform teams on supported capabilities, and operations teams on recovery and support.

A responsibility diagram should describe who makes a decision, who performs a change, who reviews evidence, and who responds when the component fails. Do not use a job title as a substitute for those agreements.

**Illustrative scenario:** a shared deployment pattern is proposed for several services. The architect can define common requirements and alternatives. Application owners assess data and interface compatibility. Platform owners assess implementation and support. Security owners review privileges. Reliability owners assess service evaluation and recovery. The final design needs these perspectives to agree on concrete responsibilities.

## Move through evidence, not title changes

A DevOps engineer moving toward architecture can begin by documenting a real delivery constraint, comparing options, piloting a change, and measuring the result. A cloud engineer may need to deepen application delivery and build-trust knowledge. An SRE may already understand failure and recovery but need more experience coordinating build, promotion, and adoption across teams.

Conversely, an architect moving into implementation-heavy work should demonstrate that the design can be translated into maintainable code, configuration, and operational procedures. Architectural vocabulary does not replace execution skills.

Review the [curriculum](curriculum.md) against your existing evidence. Skip a foundation only when you can explain and demonstrate it, not because your current title sounds senior.

## Compare an actual opportunity

Ask which systems the role owns, who its users are, how decisions are approved, how much implementation is expected, whether incident or on-call participation is required, and what success evidence is used. Clarify support responsibilities, scope across teams, and the balance between advice and operational ownership.

Do not infer compensation, seniority, authority, or workload from the title alone. This directory provides a learning framework rather than location-specific hiring guidance.

## Build a transferable portfolio

Keep a current-state assessment, requirements, options, decision records, trust boundaries, validation plan, pilot evidence, recovery results, and handover documentation. Explain which parts were implemented and which remain conceptual. Remove confidential information before publication.

A credible portfolio shows how feedback or a failed assumption changed a decision. Include limitations and unresolved risks so another engineer can assess the work honestly.
