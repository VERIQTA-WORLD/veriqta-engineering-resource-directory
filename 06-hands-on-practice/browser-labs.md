# Browser Labs

Use a hosted environment when you want to begin an exercise without assembling the whole lab yourself.

## Pick a working environment

| Open | What you can find | Access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Killercoda](https://killercoda.com/) | Choose a browser scenario or playground with a clear task and prerequisites. | Free and paid options; session limits |
| [SadServers](https://sadservers.com/) | Investigate a broken system and explain the evidence behind your fix. | Free trials and paid plans |
| [Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security) | Choose a vulnerability explanation and its authorized training lab. | Free learning and labs; account for progress |
| [Exercism](https://exercism.org/) | Select a programming-language track and solve small exercises. | Free; account needed for platform features |
| [Cisco DevNet Sandbox](https://developer.cisco.com/site/sandbox/) | Inspect available networking labs and their reservation requirements. | Lab catalog; account and availability conditions |
| [Juniper vLabs](https://jlabs.juniper.net/vlabs/) | Explore vendor-provided network topologies and lab access. | Account, reservation, and availability conditions apply |

## Make the exercise useful

Choose one scenario, read its prerequisites, and write down the result it expects before starting. Keep the observations that show why the task succeeded, rather than only its completion indicator.

**Environment and cleanup:** End the session and check whether the platform retains files or external resources; never paste production credentials into a shared environment.
